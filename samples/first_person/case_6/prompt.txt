I stand perched on a rugged, jagged cliffside made of coarse, grey-brown granite. Below me, a deep, precipitous canyon carves through the earth, its sheer walls composed of massive, vertical rock faces textured with cracks, crevices, and patches of mossy green. The steep slopes are sporadically dotted with hardy, dark green shrubs and small trees clinging to the stone. Sunlight illuminates the upper reaches of the canyon, casting soft shadows into the deeper recesses.  A powerful river surges through the canyon floor, a churning vein of white water and frothy rapids that breaks against dark, weathered boulders. The water flows steadily and relentlessly downstream, swirling into turbulent eddies and rushing through narrow passages. High above, thin clouds drift slowly across the pale blue sky, while the sparse foliage on the canyon walls sways gently in a constant breeze.
